Methods & model checks

The headline combines an election-day polling average with a fundamentals prior. The polling average uses a Kalman filter; its dashed segment projects beyond the last observed poll. Polling-bias variance is added to the poll term in the combined estimate.

Map shares are Talarico's share of votes for the two major candidates, D/(D+R). Scenario maps use joint simulation draws near the statewide 5th and 95th percentiles. Precinct and county vote counts come from a separate vote-count simulation and may imply a two-party share that differs from the map by up to about half a point.

The actual 2024 Harris result is the model's reference baseline for geographic shifts. “Reference baseline” describes its role in the model, not how the 2024 votes were obtained.

Click a number in the selected-place panel to shade every county or precinct by that measure. Red and blue map colors represent Republican and Democratic outcomes, centered on an even 50% share. Result confidence uses one purple scale from 50% (even) to 100% (certain), regardless of which candidate is favored; it is the published larger of the two candidates' modeled chances of leading. Projected margin is Talarico minus Paxton as a share of all ballots, and Brown share is Brown's share of all ballots, both as published by the model. Net vote density is the published net Talarico minus Paxton votes per square mile of full-resolution area: blue is a Talarico lead, red is a Paxton lead. Areas expected to cast under half a ballot have no margin, Brown share or density and are drawn as no data. Its color saturates at the 95th percentile of absolute net density within the selected geographic level. These are separate map views. Counts and demographic shares use percentile breaks within the selected geographic level. Estimated demographic percentages are capped at 100% for display and shading; the published CSVs retain the original estimates. Use Dark map or Light map to change the background without changing the selected measure or location.
